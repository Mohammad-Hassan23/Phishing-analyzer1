Paste a suspicious email. See exactly why it looks wrong.

This analyser reads the text of an email and looks for the tricks phishing and social-engineering attacks rely on. You get a risk score from 0 to 100, a classification, and a plain-English explanation of every warning sign that contributed - including how many points each one added.

  • Nothing leaves your device. The whole analysis happens inside this page, in your browser. There is no server, no database, no AI service, no account and no tracking.
  • Every point is explained. Each warning sign shows the exact words that triggered it and what it contributed to the score. No mystery number.
  • Built to teach, not to judge. It identifies warning signs. It can never tell you for certain that an email is malicious, and a low score is not a promise that a message is safe.

Where to find them. Headers are the technical part of an email, and they are where the SPF, DKIM and DMARC results live.
  • Gmail: open the message, click the three-dot menu, choose Show original, then copy everything.
  • Outlook / Microsoft 365: open the message, use More actions or File, then View message source or Message details.
  • Apple Mail: select the message, then View > Message > Raw Source.
  • Thunderbird: select the message, then More > View Source.

If the headers are pasted somewhere else, the tool will simply say the authentication checks were not available - it never guesses a result.

Tip: press Ctrl + Enter (Cmd + Enter on a Mac) to analyse.

Privacy notice

Your email is not sent anywhere. This is the most important design decision in the whole project, so here is how you can check it for yourself.

Analysis happens in your browser

The rules that examine your text are ordinary JavaScript files that were downloaded when you opened this page. From then on everything runs on your own device. There is no server to send anything to, because this application has no server at all.

Outbound connections are blocked by the browser

This page sends a Content-Security-Policy instruction that forbids all outbound connections (connect-src 'none'). That is not a promise, it is an enforced restriction: even if somebody later added code that tried to send your email somewhere, the browser would refuse to make the connection.

No tracking, no storage, no accounts

There is no analytics, no telemetry, no cookies and no database. Your email text is never written to storage and disappears when you close or refresh the page. The only thing this site remembers is whether you prefer the light or the dark theme.

You can verify it yourself

Open your browser's developer tools (F12), choose the Network tab, then paste an email and analyse it. You will see no requests being made. The automated self-tests also check that no file in this project contains a single network call.

What this tool is, and what it is not

Worth reading before you rely on any score, including a high one.

It cannot tell you whether an email is malicious

No tool can, and any tool that claims otherwise is misleading you. This analyser finds warning signs in text. It cannot open attachments, cannot check whether a domain was registered last week, and cannot know whether a message is genuine.

  • Never click a suspicious link or open an attachment to test it. That is exactly what the attacker is hoping for.
  • A low score is not a clean bill of health. A carefully written attack can trip none of these checks.
  • A high score is not proof of guilt. Real marketing emails use urgent language, and real invoices get flagged too.
  • Verify independently. Reach the organisation through its official website or app, or through a phone number you already hold - never one taken from the email.

How the score is calculated

Each warning sign carries a weight: how strongly that one sign, on its own, suggests phishing. The weights are combined as independent pieces of evidence rather than simply added together.

score = 100 × (1 − (1−w₁) × (1−w₂) × … × (1−wₙ))

So one 25-point warning sign gives a score of 25, three of them give 58 and six give 82 - while twenty weak signals can never reach a false 100. Every report shows each warning sign's own contribution to the total, measured by recalculating the score with that sign removed. Because the signs overlap slightly, those contributions do not add up to exactly the total, and the report says so.

Two further rules guard against obviously wrong answers. If two or more critical warning signs are present the result is raised to High Risk, and if one is present it is raised to at least Suspicious. Whenever that happens, the report explains that it happened and what the raw score was.
